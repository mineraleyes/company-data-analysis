# Stage 1 — The numbers

*1,079 mining companies. Size and trading as at 30 June 2026; commodity and property as at 31 July 2026.*

## Headline

**TSX holds 17% of the issuers and 92.8% of the market cap. These are two different populations sharing a sector label.**

| | TSX | TSXV |
|---|---|---|
| Issuers | 183 | 896 |
| Share of own board | 8% | 60% |
| Total market cap | C$1,053.6B | C$82.0B |
| Median market cap | C$1,080.3M | C$18.7M |
| Value traded H1 2026 | C$427.0B | C$19.9B |

Mining is a minor sector on the TSX and the **dominant** one on the TSXV.

## Concentration

**Half the TSX mining sector is ten companies. The TSXV is genuinely flat by comparison.**

- TSX top 10 = **54.8%** of TSX mining market cap
- Agnico Eagle alone = **10.6%**
- TSXV top 10 = **22.8%**

Only two TSXV miners exceed C$2B (Artemis Gold, Sigma Lithium).

The median producer is **38× the size of the median explorer**. Producers are also the only group not concentrated on one board — 79 on the TSX against 50 on the TSXV — while explorers are 90% TSXV.

**Stage separates outcomes better than board does.** Median return since listing:

| | Companies priced | Median return |
|---|---|---|
| Producer | 79 | **+136%** |
| Royalty / streamer | 22 | +21% |
| Explorer | 471 | −59% |
| Shell | 23 | −66% |

The board split says TSX beats TSXV. This says the same thing more precisely: the TSX is where the producers are. A TSXV producer and a TSX producer are closer to each other than either is to an explorer on the same board.

**What the labels mean.** *Producer* is a fact — the company reported revenue above C$1M. *Royalty / streamer* comes from TMX's own flag. *Shell* means no commodity and no property disclosed. *Explorer* is everything else, so it is a residual rather than a positive finding. *Unknown* is 5 companies Yahoo has no record of at all.

**There is no developer bucket, and that is a source limit rather than a choice.** Developer — a proven deposit being permitted and built — is a real and commercially interesting stage, but across all 1,079 Yahoo business summaries the word "feasibility" appears once and "construction" three times. The summaries name the commodity, not the project stage. Building the bucket anyway would have meant presenting a dozen lucky keyword matches as a count, so Seabridge and Skeena sit in Explorer alongside companies a hundredth their size. Market cap is the honest way to tell those apart.

## Size distribution

**57% of the TSXV mining board is below C$25M and collectively worth C$4.2B — a rounding error against the TSX's C$984B in the top band.**

{{chart:size}}

**The boards overlap:** 26 TSX names sit below C$100M and 174 TSXV names sit above it. Board is not a clean size proxy.

Market caps span six orders of magnitude, from C$16k to C$112B.

## Commodity

**This is a gold sector with a copper problem attached. 658 of 1,079 companies have gold exposure — 61% of the entire universe.**

{{chart:commodity}}

**Most companies are polymetallic** — 462 hold more than one commodity, 525 hold exactly one, 92 disclose none. There are 132 distinct commodity sets, but they're concentrated: Gold alone (286), Gold+Copper (113) and Gold+Silver (74) cover 44% of the universe.

**Two commodities are almost entirely a TSXV story.** Lithium is 97 TSXV against 7 TSX; rare earths 32 against 3. These are the speculative frontier — huge company counts, tiny market caps. Lithium's 104 companies are worth C$16.5B combined, less than a fifth of uranium's 50 companies at C$95.4B.

**35 royalty and streaming companies hold C$161.9B — 14% of sector market cap.** They own no mines. Any per-company comparison that treats them as operators is distorted.

## Geography — where the assets are

**Only 513 of 1,079 companies hold Canadian property, against 975 headquartered in Canada. Vancouver is a financing hub, not a mining district.**

{{chart:assets}}

**Of the 650 BC-headquartered companies, only 314 (48%) hold any Canadian property.** 159 hold Latin American assets, 150 US, 38 African. Ontario is the same story — 102 of 201.

**Sector-wide, 514 of 1,079 companies (48%) hold no assets in the region they're based in**, and at country level 452 hold no property in their country of domicile.

Top asset jurisdictions: BC (168), ON (149), QC (125), Nevada (111), Mexico (88), Peru (53), Yukon (53), Saskatchewan (47), Argentina (46), Chile (41).

Most companies are single-jurisdiction — 668 of them. Versamet Royalties is the widest at 15, Barrick at 11.

## What happens after listing

**The median mining listing loses money from week one and never recovers. By ten years it is down 82%, and 83% of them are underwater.**

Share-price return against the average of a company's first five trading days.

| After listing | Median return | Share negative |
|---|---|---|
| 1 week | −1.2% | 56% |
| 3 months | −10.3% | 62% |
| 1 year | −26.7% | 65% |
| 2 years | −47.9% | 73% |
| 5 years | −66.5% | 74% |
| 10 years | −82.3% | 83% |

**The two boards are different asset classes.** The TSX median is roughly flat for a full year — −0.4% at 12 months — before declining. The TSXV is negative from week one and reaches **−96% at fifteen years**, with 94% of companies below their listing price.

**Consolidation is the mechanism.** 227 of 595 priced companies (38%) have done a reverse split. Their median return is −91%; companies that never consolidated are at **+10%**. A company does not roll back its shares five times because things are going well.

**But the median hides an enormous spread.** At five years the middle half of TSXV companies runs from −89% to −18%, while the top decile is up 118%. It is a lottery-ticket distribution: most lose nearly everything, a minority make multiples.

Based on 595 of 1,079 companies — 222 have no listing date to anchor to and 262 have no usable price history near it. **All of them are survivors**: companies that failed outright are absent from the source file entirely, so the real distribution is worse than this.

## The disclosure floor

**51 companies disclose neither a commodity nor a property.** Median market cap C$7.4M — but median turnover 0.128, so they are trading actively. Listed as miners, with nothing on record about what or where they mine.

## Caveats

- **The two source files are a month apart.** Commodity and property are 31 July; everything financial is 30 June.
- **224 TSXV miners (25%) have no listing date** — cohort cuts run on 672, not 896
- **467 TSXV miners (52%) have Other or missing listing type**
- **92 companies have no commodity disclosed**, 70 no property
- **19 TSXV nulls in trading data** — not zeros
- **10 companies** in the June issuer file had left by the July mining file
- **Returns cover 595 companies**, are survivor-only, and each milestone is a smaller, more selected group than the last
- **Five companies have quotes discarded as impossible.** Yahoo back-adjusts for distributions, so a company that returned more capital per share than the share was then worth ends up with a negative adjusted price. Those quotes are dropped, which costs those companies some milestones rather than reporting a negative price
- **Stage is derived, not disclosed** — producer rests on reported revenue, explorer is the residual
- Prices come from Yahoo Finance and are adjusted for splits and dividends
